Ten checks on 3,164 field records from north-east Nigeria

Real data. IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix, Rounds 51 and 52, public files.

I ran ten rule checks on the survey records for six states. Some failures are plain errors, such as a school total that does not match its own breakdown. Others may be real, so I kept them separate and called them review flags.

  • 220 records report more school-age children than the whole roster.
  • 114 school totals do not match their own breakdown.
  • Adamawa has the highest share of hard-flagged records, 22.8%. I have not investigated why.

Limits: I used the published files, not the raw submissions, and the cut-offs are my own.

What 71 Shopify stores do with price

Real data. The public product catalogues of 71 Shopify stores, 22,956 products, pulled on one day.

I measured how stores structure their prices, not how much they sell. Round prices outnumber charm prices three to one, and most stores use compare-at prices only sparingly.

  • 63 of 71 stores use compare-at prices. The median store carries one on 19% of its products.
  • The median store has 93% of its products in stock. Ten stores are below 75%.

Limits: I picked the 71 stores, so the sample is not random. Stock is a one-day snapshot. Three stores listed no currency and are treated as USD.

The Last 40 Million: who is financially excluded in Nigeria, and why

Real data. EFInA's 2023 Access to Financial Services in Nigeria survey, used under a research licence. 26,930 adults aged 18 and over.

Six analysis modules on who is excluded and what blocks them. "Not enough money" was the barrier for rural and urban excluded adults alike, with a gap of 0.2 percentage points. That points to poverty more than distance.

  • Gender stops being significant once education, income, sector and region are controlled for.
  • Only 0.8% of excluded adults started banking more during the 2023 cash crunch.
  • The logistic regression covers 26,361 people and has a McFadden R² of 0.26.
